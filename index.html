I recently completed my B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering at Khulna University of Engineering & Technology (KUET), Bangladesh, and am keen to keep working on research in computer vision.

So far, the work has spanned generative modelling, image restoration and medical image analysis: how far diffusion models can be compressed before their samples suffer, how real-world degradations differ from the synthetic ones restoration methods learn from, and how medical imaging models can let related tasks inform one another and recognise when their own predictions are unreliable. Beneath all three is a concern with whether our evaluations measure what we believe they measure.

At KUET, I completed my undergraduate thesis under the supervision of Prof. Kazi Saeed Alam, worked with Prof. Sk. Md. Masudul Ahsan on my third-year system development project, and conducted research in the lab of Prof. Sk Imran Hossain. With him and Prof. Engelbert Mephu Nguifo of Université Clermont Auvergne, I have worked on compact diffusion models and on multi-task learning for medical image analysis.

In Progress

  • Severity-Channel Conditioning. Where a restoration network should be told how degraded its input is: at the input, in the features, at the output or in the objective. Under review, IEEE TIP. github
  • Frame timing in high-speed capture. An audit of silent frame-rate drops in 240 fps phone video, and the bias they leave in blur synthesised from it.
  • Dermoscopic hair inpainting. A benchmark of classical and learned hair removal on 16,689 ISIC 2019 pairs, where the ranking of methods changes with hair density. In preparation for CMPB.
  • Structural MRI in Alzheimer's disease. A review of 78 studies connecting how atrophy is measured, how progression is modelled and how conversion is predicted. In preparation for IEEE RBME.
  • XSecNet. 3D shape reconstruction from sparse cross-sections, constrained to agree with every slice it observes.
  • Echocardiographic ventricle reconstruction. The same problem in the clinic: recovering left-ventricle shape from sparse echocardiographic views.
  • GDO-MFAS. Alzheimer's progression as continuous dynamics, with a genetics-conditioned neural ODE that predicts time to conversion.
